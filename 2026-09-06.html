Blacklisted Inspur kept buying Nvidia's best AI chips via subsidiary

Washington blacklisted Inspur for supplying China's military, but the company's subsidiary continued shipping Nvidia's most advanced AI chips to China's leading AI firms, the New York Times reports. The case illustrates a persistent gap in the Entity List: designations on a parent company do not automatically capture subsidiaries operating under separate names.

So what: Raises the stakes on whether Washington extends or lets lapse the roughly Nov 10 2026 deadline on the suspended BIS Affiliates ('50%') Rule.

Pattern: The Affiliates Rule adopted Sept 29 2025 was designed to close exactly this subsidiary loophole but was suspended for one year from Nov 10 2025 under the Busan truce.

MAC: PLA artillery upgrades could extend reach across entire Miyako Strait

Taiwan's Mainland Affairs Council says in a new quarterly China report that munitions upgrades could extend PLA long-range rocket artillery to cover the entire Miyako Strait. PCH191 launchers (near-500km range) and the Fire Dragon 480 missile (480km), deployed by the 71st, 72nd and 73rd Group Armies along the Fujian coast since August 2022, currently cannot reach the strait's central waters roughly 600km away, but technological upgrades could close that gap.

So what: The Miyako Strait is the PLA Navy's key transit chokepoint to the Western Pacific; extended land-based rocket coverage would let Beijing contest it without deploying ships or aircraft.
