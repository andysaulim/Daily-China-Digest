US-CHINA

Bessent and Pan to Meet in Geneva as Tariff Truce Clock Ticks

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent will meet PBOC Governor Pan Gongsheng on Friday in Geneva — the first principal-level economic contact since the 90-day tariff truce announced in Shanghai. Neither side has indicated readiness to extend the pause, which expires July 12.

So what: The Geneva meeting is a temperature check, not a negotiation — watch for whether Bessent signals willingness to discuss structural issues (IP, subsidies) or keeps the agenda narrow.

Pattern: Same playbook as the 2019 G20 Osaka truce: a pause buys time but no structural resolution. Beijing is unlikely to offer concessions before the CPPCC economic work conference in June.

PLA Eastern Theater Command Activates Unannounced 72-Hour Exercise East of Taiwan

The PLA Eastern Theater Command launched a maritime and air exercise involving elements of the South Sea Fleet and East Sea Fleet, with J-20 sorties tracked departing Longtian Air Base. Taiwan's MND said it had scrambled six F-16Vs and activated its alert posture.

So what: The timing — days after Taiwan's new defense budget passed the Legislative Yuan — fits the pattern of using exercises as messaging tools. No live-fire zones declared, keeping it below escalation thresholds.

Pattern: Third unannounced exercise in 2026, up from two in all of 2025. The cadence is increasing without triggering US formal responses.

TECHNOLOGY

Xi Chairs PSC Session on AI Governance, Calls for China's 'Right to Speak' on Global Rules

Xinhua reported Xi Jinping chaired a Politburo Standing Committee study session focused on AI governance, calling on China to 'actively participate in and lead' international AI rulemaking. The session came ahead of China's formal submission to the UN AI Advisory Body.

So what: This is a norm-setting play. China is positioning its AI governance framework — built around state oversight, 'algorithm ethics,' and 'beneficial to humanity' rhetoric — as an alternative to the EU AI Act model.

Pattern: Pattern of using UN/multilateral forums to legitimize Beijing's domestic regulatory model internationally. Watch for this framing in the July G20 Digital Economy track.

Op-Eds & Think Tank Commentary
Foreign Affairs · Bonny Lin, Ivan Kanapathy
The Taiwan Contingency Planning Gap in Washington
US contingency planning for a Taiwan scenario has not kept pace with PLA capability improvements since 2022. The gap between stated deterrence posture and executable military options is wider than official assessments acknowledge.
Lin and Kanapathy draw on unclassified war-game outputs and INDOPACOM testimony to argue that US deterrence credibility over Taiwan depends on closing three specific gaps: pre-positioned logistics, allied interoperability, and sub-threshold signaling capacity. The article is notable for its frank assessment of how little the 2025 Pacific Deterrence Initiative funding has moved the needle.
Directly relevant to the Senate Armed Services markup next week — Lin is expected to testify.
CSIS · Scott Kennedy
China's Industrial Policy Is Working — and That's the Problem
Beijing's state-directed industrial policy in EVs, solar, and batteries has produced globally competitive firms — not despite market distortions but partly because of them. Western responses that ignore this dynamic will be ineffective.
Kennedy argues that the conventional framing of Chinese industrial policy as purely subsidy-driven misses the role of domestic competition among state-backed champions. The policy challenge for Washington is not just overcapacity but the emergence of genuinely competitive Chinese technology firms across multiple sectors simultaneously.
Implications for the Section 301 review and proposed reciprocal tariff architecture.
Peterson Institute for International Economics · Chad Bown, Mary Lovely
After the Truce: What Structural US-China Trade Deal Would Actually Require
A durable US-China trade deal requires addressing subsidies, state-owned enterprise advantages, and IP enforcement — none of which the 90-day tariff pause touches. The truce buys time but not resolution.
Bown and Lovely provide a detailed accounting of the structural gaps a real deal would need to close, drawing on the Phase One agreement's failures as a baseline. They estimate that achieving meaningful structural reforms would require 3–5 years of sustained negotiation even under favorable political conditions in both capitals.
Baseline reading ahead of the Bessent-Pan Geneva meeting.

Coercive Gradualism and PLA Gray-Zone Operations in the Taiwan Strait
Drawing on PLA operational doctrine documents and a systematic coding of maritime and airspace incidents 2020-2025, Fravel and Blasko argue that PLA gray-zone activity follows a doctrine of coercive gradualism — deliberately calibrated escalation designed to shift baselines without triggering formal responses.
