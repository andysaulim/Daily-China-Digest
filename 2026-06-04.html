China's AI competition strategy: Wide dispersion, cheap tokens
China is pursuing AI dominance through widely distributed, low-cost token generation rather than concentrated high-performance computing.
MERICS analysis reveals China's strategy to compete in AI through distributed computing networks that offer cheap token generation at scale. This approach allows Chinese companies to democratize access to AI capabilities while circumventing US export controls on advanced chips. The strategy leverages China's manufacturing scale and domestic market size to create alternative pathways to AI leadership that don't rely on cutting-edge semiconductor technology.
US export controls may need recalibration to address distributed AI architectures rather than focusing solely on advanced chip restrictions.

China's memory chip manufacturers will benefit as global competitors shift production capacity toward AI-specific processors.
As Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron reallocate production lines from traditional memory to AI chips, Chinese firms like YMTC and CXMT face reduced competition in standard memory markets. Western semiconductor strategies may inadvertently strengthen China's position in foundational memory technologies while pursuing AI chip leadership.

Carnegie
Trump's AI Order Won't Stymie U.S. Competition with China
President Trump's executive order on AI will not significantly impede US-China technological competition despite regulatory changes.
Carnegie analysts argue that Trump's AI executive order, while rolling back some Biden-era restrictions, maintains core export controls and security measures affecting China. The order prioritizes American innovation and competitiveness over restrictive oversight, potentially accelerating US AI development. However, fundamental technology transfer restrictions and entity list controls remain intact, preserving competitive barriers against Chinese AI advancement.
The order signals a shift toward innovation-focused rather than restriction-focused AI policy while maintaining strategic technological barriers.
